📋 Paste anything (Ctrl+V)
Screenshots and copied images land as image cards. Selections copied from Excel, PDFs, or websites (tables, formatted text) are converted to a JPEG image automatically — if that copied content happens to include a link back to a source page, it's auto-attached underneath the image as a purple-tethered sub-asset, so you never lose track of where a table or clip came from. (This depends entirely on whatever the source app put on the clipboard — plain prose selections usually won't have a link to find, and that's fine, the image still pastes normally without one.) YouTube / X-Twitter / Instagram links become live embed cards; other URLs become link chips; plain text becomes a text box.
🎥 Capture mode — for OBS (F10)
Hides the titlebar, toolbar, minimap, badges and every other piece of UI so an OBS window-capture shows only your board. Press F10 again to bring the UI back.
🔴 OBS transparent overlay — live floating elements
Your elements float over your footage with a real transparent background — like a live transparent PNG, no chroma key, nothing to remove in edit. In OBS: Sources → + → Browser, paste the link below, set Width/Height to your canvas (e.g. 1920×1080). The overlay mirrors this window live — every move, zoom, and F9 presentation step — while you keep the full app UI on your own screen. The audience never sees the app.
▶ Presentation mode (F9)
The camera glides from element to element in serial-number order — narrate while you step with ←/→. The current element gets a gold glow and a step counter shows at the bottom. Click a serial badge beforehand to change the order. Combine with F10 for clean recorded walkthroughs.
🔗 Wires & labels
Drag the gold port dot on any element edge onto another element to connect them. Click a wire to select it: add a text label, a unit tag, bend it around obstacles by dragging, or straighten/delete it from the tools cluster.
🔗 Grouping (Ctrl+G)
Select 2+ elements and press Ctrl+G (or right-click → Group selection) to lock them together — a dashed cyan box appears around the whole set. Click any member to select the entire group, drag any member to move them all together, or drag a corner of the box to scale the whole group as one unit. Ctrl+Shift+G (or right-click → Ungroup) splits them back into independent elements. This is a flat, persistent set of peers — different from Alt+drag attachments below, which build a parent/child tree instead.
📎 Sub-asset attachments (Alt+drag)
Hold Alt and drop one element onto another to mark it as belonging to that host (e.g. a source link under a photo). Shown with a dashed purple tether and dotted numbering like 1.1, 1.2. Click the tether and press Del to detach — deleting the host never deletes its children.
✏️ Image annotations
Select an image and use the floating bar below it to draw arrows, circles, and text labels directly on the image. Annotations scale with the image and appear in exports.
𝐁 Rich text: bold, italic, checklists
Double-click any text/quote/sticky to edit it — a small toolbar appears above it. B and I wrap the selected text (or drop your cursor between fresh markers if nothing's selected); the ☑ button turns the current line into a checklist item. Checked items get a strikethrough, and once you click away, clicking a checklist's own box toggles it done/undone right there on the board — no need to re-enter edit mode. Stored as plain **bold**/*italic*/☐ text under the hood, so it stays simple and portable.
🎨 Color tags
Select any element to show a 5-swatch color bar above it — pick a color to ring the element in a thick, always-visible outline (works on every element type, including images). Click the small 🎨 dot in the element's corner to hide the bar without losing the color, and click it again to bring the bar back; selecting a different element always shows its bar fresh. The ✕ on the bar itself clears the color and closes the bar in one click.
👁 Quick Look
Select any element and tap Space for a fullscreen preview — images, videos, PDFs, sound, and text all supported. Tap again to close.
#️⃣ Serial numbers
Every element gets a numbered badge. Click a badge to renumber it (others shift automatically). Serials define presentation order and can be hidden from exports.
💾 Saving & restore
Autosaves every 30 s of inactivity plus every minute (last 30 kept). Ctrl+S saves manually (last 10 kept). The Restore… button lists both sets, and lets you change the save folder — type in its search box to find a board by name across everything you've made, even across many video projects, without hunting through Explorer. The last board reopens automatically on startup.
🖼 Export PNG
Export the whole board or just what's selected, with optional transparent background. All UI chrome, port dots, and (optionally) serial badges are hidden in the output.
📐 Alignment guides
Drag or resize any element and a pink guide line appears the instant its edge or center lines up with another element — release and it snaps exactly into place. New elements now also fade/scale in instead of just popping onto the board, and new wires draw themselves in from end to end.
🧭 Smooth camera
Ctrl+scroll zoom eases toward the cursor instead of jumping. Search results and minimap clicks glide the camera to the target.
🔍 Search (Ctrl+F)
Finds text in any element — text boxes, stickies, quotes, file names, and URLs. Matches are highlighted and Enter steps through them, gliding the camera to each.
🗺 Minimap & stats
The minimap (bottom-right) shows the whole board with your current view framed — click anywhere on it to jump. The Stats button counts your assets by type, and now also surfaces a 🏷 Top words digest — the words repeated most often across your notes, so you get a quick sense of what the board is actually about at a glance. This is a simple offline word-frequency count, not real AI — no network call, no account, no cost, just counting.
🔎 Find duplicate notes
The Stats panel's 🔎 Duplicates button scans all your text/quote/sticky notes and outlines any that are identical or near-identical (ignoring extra whitespace) in blue, so copy-pasted or repeated ideas from a long brainstorm are easy to spot and clean up. Click again to clear the highlight. Also fully offline — no AI involved, just a text comparison.
🏗 Templates & layout
The Templates button starts a board from a preset structure — 10 presets (Video Outline, Character Map, Research Dump, Thumbnail Ideas, Episode Breakdown, Comparison/Versus, Tutorial/How-To, Video Series Planner, SWOT Analysis, Interview/Q&A Prep) plus a Blank Board. Every preset comes pre-numbered in reading order, so F9 presentation and Presenter Notes work immediately without you touching a single serial badge. Layout auto-arranges connected elements — click it repeatedly to cycle styles (tree, horizontal tree, radial).
🗒 Presenter Notes (like PowerPoint's speaker notes)
Right-click any asset → Commentary… to write what you'll say about it. Click the toolbar's 🗒 Notes button to open a separate window with its own title — drag it to a second monitor. It shows every numbered asset's commentary as a stack of cards, 1 through N, plus any sub-assets nested right under their host as 1.1, 1.2 (Alt+drag attachments — see below; a sub-asset can't itself hold further sub-assets, so numbering never goes deeper than one dot). Whichever asset you've selected, or whichever one F9 presentation is on, lights up bold with a gold border and auto-scrolls into view while every other card stays dimmed but readable — so stepping through assets with the arrow keys/Page Down moves your place in the notes too. Its own toolbar lets you flip to a light background, change the font family, and bump the text size up or down — all remembered next time you open it. The same toolbar has a 📖 Log / ✏ Edit switch: it always opens read-only in Log mode so you never accidentally start typing mid-presentation, but flipping to Edit turns every card's commentary into a text box you can rewrite right there — changes flow straight back to the board's actual Commentary data (same as editing via right-click), it just autosaves as you pause typing. Because it's a genuinely separate OS window, an OBS Window Capture of the board never shows it — only you see it. Assets with commentary get a small teal dot next to their serial badge — click the dot to jump straight back into editing it.
